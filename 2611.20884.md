<div id="title">

# Orbital decomposition and evolution of \texttt{LEGA-C}-like galaxies in \texttt{IllustrisTNG} from $z=1$ to $z=0$

</div>
<div id="comments">

[![arXiv](https://img.shields.io/badge/arXiv-2611.20884-b31b1b.svg)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.20884)<mark>Appeared on: 2026-11-17</mark> -  _Accepted for publication in A&A on 27/10/2026_

</div>
<div id="authors">

P. Kumar, et al. -- incl., <mark>A. Pillepich</mark>

</div>
<div id="abstract">

**Abstract:** Stellar orbits encode the dynamical history of galaxies and provide a physically motivated link between galaxy structures and assembly processes. Although orbit-based decompositions are well established in the Local Universe, their evolution across cosmic time, especially at higher redshifts, remains poorly explored. We investigate the orbital structure of \texttt{LEGA-C-like} galaxies in the \texttt{TNG100} simulation from $z=1$ to $z=0$ to identify how stellar mass, star formation activity, environment, merger history, gas content, and ex situ stellar mass growth shape their orbital transformation. We decomposed the stellar orbital structure of simulated galaxies into cold, warm, hot, and counter-rotating (CR) components using time-averaged orbital circularities. This provides a theoretical basis for future comparisons with Schwarzschild-based orbital decompositions of observed galaxies. We analyzed the galaxy populations at $z=1$ and $z=0$ , then tracked the descendants of individual $z=1$ galaxies to quantify their orbital evolution over this time. We find a strong mass dependence of orbital structure at $z=0$ as well as at $z=1$ . Galaxies below $\log(M_\star/M_\odot)\sim11.2$ have a combined warm and cold orbital fraction of $\sim70\%$ , whereas more massive systems have a hot-orbit fraction of $\sim60\%$ . These trends are consistent with assembly pathways driven predominantly by wet and dry major mergers, respectively. Tracking the same galaxies to $z=0$ reveals a systematic, but mass-dependent, evolution toward lower rotational support: At both low and high stellar masses, the hot-orbit fraction increases, whereas the relative change is greater in low-mass galaxies. The survival of cold structure depends strongly on the star formation state at $z=1$ ; star-forming progenitors retain substantially larger cold fractions than the quiescent progenitors. At both redshifts, the orbital fractions show a rise (decline) in hot and CR  (cold and warm) orbits with the ex situ stellar mass fraction at a fixed stellar mass. These results provide the first evolutionary framework for interpreting orbit-based decompositions of galaxies at the \texttt{LEGA-C} epoch. Stellar orbital fractions quantify the morphological and dynamical transformation of galaxies and show systematic evolutionary trends at the population level. Individual galaxies, however, follow diverse evolutionary pathways. Comparisons to future Schwarzschild-based decompositions of observed high-redshift galaxies can use these results to interpret the internal physical structure of galaxy populations and constrain theoretical models of galaxy evolution.

**Figure 7. -** Stellar orbital fractions within $2R_{1/2}$ as a function of stellar mass for \texttt{TNG100} galaxies at $z=1$(left) and $z=0$(right), for the \texttt{survived LEGA-C-like}(solid) and \texttt{whole/extended LEGA-C-like}(dashed) samples. The lines show the median orbital fractions, with shaded regions indicating the $1\sigma$ scatter. Top row: At both redshifts, orbital structure depends strongly on stellar mass: lower-mass galaxies are dominated by warm and cold orbits, whereas massive systems become dominated by hot orbits. From $z=1$ to $z=0$, the hot fraction increases, while the cold and warm components decline, indicating a systematic evolution toward dynamically hotter stellar structures. Middle row: Evolution is also shown to be dependent strongly on star formation activity: at fixed stellar mass, \texttt{quiescent} galaxies generally have higher (lower) hot (cold) fractions than \texttt{star-forming} galaxies, with the difference becoming particularly pronounced by $z=0$. Bottom row: In contrast, \texttt{central} and \texttt{satellite} galaxies follow broadly similar mass-dependent trends. (*fig:fraction_plot_z1_z0*)

**Figure 8. -** Evolution of the stellar orbital fractions from $z=1$ to $z=0$ for all galaxies (top), star-forming galaxies (middle), and quiescent galaxies (bottom) binned by their $z=0$ stellar mass. The upper row shows the corresponding mean standard deviation values ($\sigma$) of the orbital fractions (see Appendix \ref{OrbitalEvolution_Stellar mass_Sigma}) . The orbital components show strongly mass dependent evolution: in the most massive bins, the hot (warm) component shows an increase (decline) from $z=1$ to $z=0$, whereas in the less massive bins, the warm components remain higher than the hot components across redshift. Meanwhile, the cold components continue to decline from $z=1$ to $z=0$, from their highest to their lowest fractions. Comparing star-forming and quiescent galaxies, we find that star-forming galaxies retain larger cold fractions, particularly at lower stellar masses. (*fig:orbital_evolution*)

**Figure 4. -** \texttt{TNG100} galaxy populations from $z=1$ to $z=0$.
Middle panels: sSFR versus stellar mass at $z=1$(left) and $z=0$(right). Galaxies are classified relative to the SFMS as star-forming or quiescent. The SFMS normalization also declines, with more galaxies lying below it. This transition is strongest among satellites, where quiescent systems dominate by $z=0$, consistent with environmental quenching after infall. The simultaneous growth of the quiescent central population, however, indicates that quenching also involves processes independent of environment.
