<div id="title">

# A dust-reddened quasar in a $\cii$-luminous host at $z=6.55$

</div>
<div id="comments">

[![arXiv](https://img.shields.io/badge/arXiv-2610.13758-b31b1b.svg)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.13758)<mark>Appeared on: 2026-10-13</mark> -  _11 figures, 2 tables, including appendices_

</div>
<div id="authors">

D. Yang, et al. -- incl., <mark>E. Bañados</mark>

</div>
<div id="abstract">

**Abstract:** Most known quasars at $\(z>6\)$ were selected through their rest-frame UV emission. Only a small number of reddened quasars have been identified at these redshifts, leaving their nuclear and host-galaxy properties poorly constrained. We present JWST/NIRSpec spectroscopy and ALMA band 6 observations of the quasar $\yssl$ at $\(z=6.5488\)$ , revealing its dust-reddened nature and jointly characterizing its nucleus and host galaxy. The continuum is best described by an SMC-like extinction law with $\(E(B-V)=0.20\pm0.03\)$ . Three absorption systems detected in $\ion{N}{v}$ , $\ion{Si}{iv}$ , and $\ion{C}{iv}$ are blueshifted by approximately $\(900\)$ -- $\(3600 \kms\)$ and likely trace an ionized nuclear outflow. The broad $\ion{Mg}{ii}$ line and extinction-corrected continuum yield a black-hole mass of $\(\log(M_{\rm BH}/\msun)=9.04\pm0.42\)$ , a bolometric luminosity of $\(L_{\rm bol}\simeq1.1\times10^{47} {\rm erg s^{-1}}\)$ , and an Eddington ratio of $\(\lambda_{\rm Edd}\simeq0.8\)$ . ALMA observations further reveal its host galaxy to be among the most $\cii$ -luminous known at $\(z>6\)$ , with $\(\lcii=(7.21\pm0.25)\times10^9 \lsun\)$ . For a fiducial dust temperature of 47 K and emissivity index $\(\beta=1.6\)$ , the dust continuum implies $\(\ltir=8.9\times10^{12} \lsun\)$ and $\(M_{\rm dust}=4.0\times10^8 \msun\)$ . The inferred dynamical mass places $\yssl$ in the region of the $\(M_{\rm BH}\)$ -- $\(M_{\rm dyn}\)$ plane occupied by high-redshift blue quasars. These observations reveal a moderately reddened, rapidly accreting quasar in a luminous, dust-rich host during reionization. Its measured nuclear and host-galaxy properties provide an observational reference for identifying and characterizing similar systems in future systematic searches with wide-area infrared surveys.
