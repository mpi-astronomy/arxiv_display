|| S. A. Cronin, et al. -- incl., <mark>J. Smith</mark>, <mark>F. Walter</mark> |
|*Appeared on*| *2026-11-19*|
|*Comments*| *18 pages, 5 main figures, 4 appendix figures, submitted to ApJL*|
|**Abstract**|            We present the first spatially resolved study of polycyclic aromatic hydrocarbon (PAH) evolution in the galactic superwind of the nearby (d~3.5Mpc) galaxy NGC 253. Using JWST MIRI and NIRCam, we image ~$\pm$2kpc along the galaxy minor axis, capturing both sides of the starburst-driven wind at ~0.05''-0.375'' (~0.85-6.4pc) resolution and providing one of the most detailed views of the wind to date. We target PAH emission at 3.3um, 7.7um, and 11.3um, revealing PAH-bright filamentary streamers along the limb-brightened edges of the outflow cones out to at least a deprojected distance r~0.5kpc, beyond which they likely become confused with the spiral arms. In the southwest (SW) and northeast (NE) streamers, PAH intensity declines faster than $r^{-2}$, suggesting either PAH destruction or a decrease in gas column density or dust-to-gas ratio rather than a response to declining illumination alone. Band ratios trace changes in PAH properties, with the 11.3/7.7um ratio increasing with distance in the SW and northwest (NW) streamers and 3.3/7.7um and 3.3/11.3um decreasing in the SW and NE streamers, indicating larger, more neutral PAHs farther from the starburst. Given the thermal and dynamical properties of the gas in the SW streamer, cool gas is expected to survive acceleration in the outflow and enable PAH shielding. Compared with those of the prototypical starburst M82, the NGC 253 band ratios indicate faster evolution in PAH size. Thus, the NGC 253 wind may represent an earlier stage of outflow evolution in which small PAHs are destroyed near the starburst within the first few Myr.         |

| [![arXiv](https://img.shields.io/badge/arXiv-2611.23492-b31b1b.svg)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.23492) | **JEPA for astronomy: multi-survey spectral alignment puts stellar parameters on the leading principal axes**  |
|| X. Zhao, R. F. Wyse, <mark>Y.-S. Ting</mark>, A. S. Szalay |
|*Appeared on*| *2026-11-19*|
|*Comments*| *15 pages, 5 figures, 2 tables. Submitted to AAS journals. Comments welcome*|
|**Abstract**|            Spectra from different surveys carry different systematics and are not calibrated against one another. Multi-survey foundation models for stellar spectra are then built by aligning embeddings of the same star observed across surveys. Most in astronomy follow the recipes established for language and vision, aligning with a contrastive loss that separates every star from the others. However, this does not ensure that the stellar physics is encoded in a form that a simple read-out can recover. Holding the cross-survey architecture fixed and varying only the alignment loss, we measure what an objective encodes from the principal-component geometry of the Gaia BP/RP (XP) spectra and from a linear probe of the stellar parameters. In a properly aligned embedding, the leading two principal components form a plane that recovers an analog of the Kiel diagram, the third component tracks [Fe/H], and [alpha/Fe] varies coherently across the plane, such that the chemically defined Galactic disks and accreted populations occupy distinct loci. We show that legibility, the ease of reading the information out, is highest for a training objective that never pushes stars apart, and instead constrains only the overall distribution of each survey's embeddings (JEPA). JEPA also exceeds the contrastive alternative at the same architecture, and the performance improves further as more surveys (Gaia XP+LAMOST LRS+DESI+APOGEE) are included. Predicted abundances for 8.0 million XP giants reproduce the main [alpha/Fe]-[Fe/H] structure of the Milky Way. These results indicate that the choice of alignment objective, not alignment alone, determines whether stellar labels are linearly recoverable.         |

|**Abstract**|            The relation between stellar mass and gas-phase metallicity (MZR) is a key tracer of the baryon cycle in galaxies. JWST now gives access to rest-optical emission lines at z > 3, enabling unprecedented constraints on the chemical enrichment and the conditions of the interstellar medium of galaxies in the early Universe. In this work, we present the MZR at $z=3.8-6.6$ from stacked NIRCam/grism spectra of 1748 H$\alpha$-selected star-forming galaxies compiled over an area of 320 arcmin$^2$. The simple H$\alpha$ flux-limited selection allows us to measure representative [NII]$\lambda$6584/H$\alpha$ and [SII]$\lambda\lambda$6716,6731/ H$\alpha$ ratios in stacks spanning $\rm log(M_\star/M_\odot)=8-10.2$, extending the upper mass range of previous high-redshift MZR measurements by $\sim1$ dex. Gas-phase metallicities are derived from these ratios using strong-line calibrations recently derived from high-redshift galaxies. The[NII]-based MZR has a slope $\gamma = 0.42^{+0.10}_{-0.07}$ and $12+\rm log(O/H) = 7.94^{+0.06}_{-0.09}$ at $\rm log(M_\star/M_\odot) = 9$, in broad agreement with recent empirical studies and hydrodynamical simulations. The [SII]-based relation yields a consistent slope and a $\sim0.3$ dex higher normalization, within the systematic uncertainties of strong-line calibrations. From the resolved [SII] doublet we infer a median electron density of $n_e = 184^{+226}_{-169}\,\rm cm^{-3}$. Combining [NII] and [SII], we indirectly estimate the N/O ratio and find that it follows the trend expected from primary and secondary nitrogen production, with no evidence for nitrogen enhancement in the HII region emission that dominates our galaxies. Our results provide a benchmark for the chemical enrichment and ISM conditions of typical star-forming galaxies at $z\sim5$ across a wide mass range.         |

|**Abstract**|            Several scenarios have been proposed to explain the properties of Little Red Dots (LRDs), invoking either heavy obscuration by dense neutral gas, super-Eddington accretion, or an ionized gas cocoon that produces electron scattering. Radio observations can distinguish between these scenarios. Radio emission is largely unaffected by neutral gas and dust obscuration, whereas free-free absorption can suppress it in the presence of large free electron columns. Here, we investigate the origin of radio emission from the local LRD J1047. We jointly analyze its radio data and the broad H$\alpha$ emission from optical spectroscopy to test the predictions of different physical scenarios. We find that the observed radio luminosity must have an AGN origin, and it is consistent with that of a standard radio-quiet AGN obscured by dense neutral gas. Pure super-Eddington accretion underpredicts the radio luminosity by a factor of $\sim 30$, although a combination of super-Eddington accretion and obscuration remains viable. Interestingly, the statistically preferred model for the H$\alpha$ profile is an exponential fit, which is often associated with electron scattering in LRDs. This interpretation implies an electron column density of $N_e=2.4\times10^{24}\rm~cm^{-2}$ and a strong free-free absorption ($\tau_{\rm ff,5GHz}\sim 10^6$), incompatible with the radio detection. This tension suggests that the exponential profiles of J1047 more likely trace a stratified broad line region rather than electron scattering, with potential implications for virial black-hole mass estimates also in high-$z$ LRDs. Less likely, the radio emission may escape through a partially covering ionized medium or originate outside the putative scattering cocoon.         |

|**Abstract**|            Gaia Science Alerts provide an all-sky stream of photometric transients, including events consistent with gravitational microlensing. For such events, forthcoming epoch astrometry from the Gaia mission offers the possibility of testing photometric lens mass predictions. While microlensing parallax inferred from the photometric light curve can constrain the event geometry, a direct lens mass measurement additionally requires the angular Einstein radius, which may be accessible through astrometric microlensing. We aim to construct a pre-Data-Release-4 (DR4) sample of Gaia microlensing events for which the available photometry provides a measurable microlensing-parallax signal. The inferred lens masses and distances remain strongly prior-dependent without a direct angular Einstein radius measurement.
