|| <mark>B. Pennell</mark>, <mark>J. Müller-Horn</mark>, <mark>S. Shahaf</mark>, <mark>H.-W. Rix</mark>, <mark>K. El-Badry</mark>, <mark>J. Li</mark> |
|*Appeared on*| *2026-11-18*|
|*Comments*| *Submitted to A&A November 16th, 2026. Comments welcome*|
|**Abstract**|            Brown dwarfs occupy the mass range between stars and planets, populations whose dominant formation channels differ, which leave measurable differences in the orbital eccentricity and host-star metallicity distributions. We assemble a sample of astrometric binaries with main sequence primaries and brown dwarf companions at $\mathcal{O}$(AU) separations from $\textit{Gaia}$ DR3. The observable photocenter motions for such binaries are degenerate between a genuinely low-mass companion and a near-equal-mass binary. We break this degeneracy using $\textit{Gaia}$ XP spectrophotometry and follow-up FEROS spectroscopy, which removes removes ${\sim}70\%$ of initial low-mass-function candidates as likely near-twin binaries and leaves residual contamination of only a few percent. The resulting 368 systems, with masses and metallicities determined from the XP spectra and a selection function forward-modelled with $\texttt{Gaiamock}$, form the largest homogeneous sample of brown dwarf companions with astrometric orbits. We find the eccentricity distribution to be well described by a normal distribution. This is comparable to eccentricities of stellar companions at the same periods and higher than those of giant planets, with no significant dependence on companion mass ($\alpha=-0.13\pm0.09$) or period ($\beta=0.10\pm0.05$). The host-star metallicity distribution is likewise stellar-like, extending to [M/H]$=-0.5$, in contrast to the predominantly super-solar metallicity hosts of giant planets. After accounting for selection, the companion-mass distribution over all brown dwarf masses is consistent with an extension of stellar-binary demographics. Brown dwarf companions at AU separations down to at least $20\,M_J$ therefore appear to form predominantly through a binary-star-like channel. A possible transition to planet-like formation at lower masses cannot be located with the present sample.         |

|| <mark>R. Hoppe</mark> |
|*Appeared on*| *2026-11-18*|
|*Comments*| *Accepted for publication in J. Quant. Spectrosc. Radiat. Transf. 10 pages, 2 figures, 5 tables. Fortran module implementing the QLG members included as ancillary file*|
|**Abstract**|            The Voigt function is evaluated repeatedly in spectral synthesis, and several widely used implementations carry an absolute error floor. We construct QLG, a family of branchless, single-precision approximations, separable in the damping parameter $a$. QLG is named for its three ingredients: Lorentzian and Gaussian generators $L$ and $G$, and an envelope $q$ that multiplies every fitted correction and vanishes at both endpoints. The leading Lorentzian wing amplitude is fixed analytically. Three members reach observed maximum full-profile relative errors of $6.5\times10^{-3}$, $9.2\times10^{-4}$, and $7\times10^{-5}$ over $a\in[10^{-9},10^{9}]$ and $|v|\le10^{5}$. Extended-domain and randomized checks support these error levels. For the tested SIU, MOOG, and Humlicek w4 implementations, the relative error instead approaches 100% towards small values of the damping parameter. We compare synthesis-weighted costs on an Apple M1 and an AMD EPYC 9554 using gfortran 15, and propagate errors through solar-dwarf, red-supergiant, and M-dwarf syntheses. The least expensive member costs 0.98-1.05 times as much as the tabulated Synspec kernel on the M1 and 1.11-1.20 times as much on the EPYC, with a maximum flux error smaller by a factor of 3.6. The intermediate member costs less than every tested arithmetic kernel and holds the flux error below $10^{-3}$. The most accurate member costs 1.6-3.0 times less than w4 and gives 3.4-5.0 times smaller maximum flux errors in the tested syntheses.         |

|**Abstract**|            Omega Centauri (NGC 5139, $\omega$ Cen) is the most massive globular cluster in the Milky Way and the likely stripped nucleus of an accreted dwarf galaxy. Extensive HST observations have uncovered many of its peculiar features, including complex subpopulations and kinematic evidence for an intermediate-mass black hole via central fast-moving stars. High-precision astrometric follow-up is essential to constrain the orbits of these stars. We present oMEGACat-JWST, a deep photometric and astrometric catalog of $\omega$ Cen's core and surroundings that extends our previous oMEGACat efforts to near-infrared wavelengths. The superior sensitivity and angular resolution of the James Webb Space Telescope (JWST) provide significant astrometric and photometric improvements, particularly for faint stars. Our catalog is based on a custom reduction of NIRCam and NIRISS imaging across three JWST programs extending out to two half-light radii (10'). In total, our catalog contains approximately 677 000 sources. In the region overlapping with oMEGACat, we achieve high relative completeness (> 90% of HST sources recovered) while detecting over 86 000 new sources below HST's detection limit. Astrometric tests demonstrate a single-image precision as good as 0.5% of a NIRCam pixel (0.15 mas) at mF115W=18; at mF115W=23 the precision is still 1 mas. Color-magnitude diagrams based on our data show the well-known split between the primordial Helium and the Helium enriched populations and are particularly sensitive to light-element (CNO) variations of faint stars. oMEGACat-JWST provides the most complete photometric catalog of $\omega$ Cen's core to date. Combined with HST data, it enables multi-wavelength subpopulation analyses. Together with future monitoring observations, its high astrometric precision will improve our understanding of the kinematics of the central fast-moving stars in $\omega$ Cen.         |

|**Abstract**|            Little red dots (LRDs) combine V-shaped ultraviolet-optical continua, broad Balmer emission, and weak X-rays. The nearby analog SDSS J102530.29+140207.3, known as Lord/Egg, offers detailed spectroscopy and polarimetry. We test one active galactic nucleus (AGN) geometry by fitting its ultraviolet-to-infrared continuum with an inclination-dependent accretion-disk spectral energy distribution (SED), young stars, and a clumpy dusty screen. The same SED is used in Cloudy calculations of a radially stratified, virialized broad-line region (BLR). Thus, a dusty, high-inclination accretion system can explain Lord/Egg within one connected geometry.         |

|**Abstract**|            The discovery with JWST of accreting black holes (BHs) at z=4-9 in numbers far exceeding pre-JWST expectations, many of them overmassive relative to their hosts and linked to the enigmatic Little Red Dots (LRDs), raises the question of how early BHs form and grow. Hierarchical assembly predicts that galaxy mergers drive gas to galactic nuclei and bring BHs together, leaving a direct observational signature: dual AGN at tens of kpc separations and sub-kpc to few-kpc "duets" in post-merger galaxies, which are the progenitors of the low-frequency GW sources targeted by pulsar timing arrays and LISA. |**Abstract**|            The incidence of radio-loud active galactic nuclei (RLAGN) and their energetic output via AGN feedback are crucial in understanding the evolution of galaxies and the large-scale structure of the Universe. In the local Universe, the incidence of RLAGN has been shown to correlate strongly with host galaxy properties, particularly stellar mass. Using the large-area survey capabilities provided by Euclid and the LOw Frequency ARray (LOFAR), we present the first study of the RLAGN incidence--stellar mass relation to $z=3.0$. We perform a stringent analysis accounting for cross-matching reliability and stellar-mass incompleteness.
