|| E. Collaboration, et al. -- incl., <mark>K. Jahnke</mark> |
|*Appeared on*| *2026-11-16*|
|*Comments*| *30 pages, 13 figures*|
|**Abstract**|            Euclid is expected to discover ~$10^5$ galaxy-galaxy strong gravitational lenses across ~14,000deg$^2$, requiring automated searches with high completeness and low false-positive rates. Despite advances in deep learning, the effects of training-set composition, image representation, and preprocessing on survey-scale detection remain poorly understood. We present an ablation study of the convolutional neural network used in the ECLIPSE strong-lens detection pipeline, evaluating different training configurations on a fixed test set. Using simulated and real Euclid data, we compare VIS-only and pseudo-RGB representations, FITS and JPEG formats, training-set composition, hard-negative selection, and multi-stage training. We also assess ensembling on ~67 million DR1 cutouts. Training-set composition and strategy strongly affect high-Recall performance. Omitting simulation based pre-training reduces high-Recall weighted average Precision by 83.7% for VIS-only and 74.5% for pseudo-RGB models. Negative-sample selection also substantially affects performance, while FITS yields modest gains over JPEG at high Recall (~2-5%). On DR1, the logit-mean ensemble improves average Precision by 28.0% and 39.9% over the individual VIS-only and pseudo-RGB models. Among ~70,000 candidates selected for visual inspection, it recovers 11,256 unique grade A (secure) and B (probable) lenses, compared with 9650 and 10,395 for the individual models. The branches show complementary ranking preferences, particularly for lenses benefiting from near-infrared information. Careful training-set design, realistic negatives, and multi-band ensembling improve survey-scale searches. Our final ensemble achieves ~80% Recall at the operational selection threshold, outperforming ablation models with higher global classification metrics and highlighting the need to optimise performance for realistic survey conditions.[Abridged]         |

|**Abstract**|            The ESA satellite mission \Euclid was launched in July 2023 and started its regular observing mode in February 2024. The data from the \Euclid instruments Visible Camera (VIS) and the Near IR Spectrometer and Photometer (NISP) are complemented by ground-based imaging data in the $u$, $g$ $r$, $i$, and $z$ bands to enable the determination of photometric redshifts and other galaxy properties. This paper describes the MERge processing function (MER PF) that has been developed and run for the Euclid Data Release 1 (DR1). The aim of the MER PF is to detect objects in the \Euclid imaging data, measure their properties, and combine them into a single multi-wavelength catalogue. In this paper we illustrate the design of the MER PF and introduce the different photometric methods applied to the data, which are Kron total flux, aperture photometry on Point Spread Functions (PSF) -matched images, template fitting photometry, and Sérsic fitting photometry. We also present the various morphological measurements and quality flags that improve the scientific content of the output catalogues and better characterize the detected sources. We illustrate the processing and measurements that help to validate the MER PF results, which is an essential step to qualify the output for the public DR1 release. We discuss the global assessment of the photometric homogeneity and provide an internal comparison of our processing results. In addition, we give an extensive overview of the DR1 output data and the various processing scenarios         |

|| E. Collaboration, et al. -- incl., <mark>K. Jahnke</mark> |
|*Appeared on*| *2026-11-16*|
|*Comments*| **|
|**Abstract**|            Strong gravitational lens modelling requires the lens light, lensed source light, and contaminants to be separated in each image before mass modelling codes can fit the system. Within Euclid this separation has so far been done by hand, which does not scale to the more than 15,000 candidates in the Euclid Lens Identification Pipeline and Scientific Exploration (ECLIPSE) catalogue of Data Release 1 (DR1). We present AstroVink-TREX , a neural network that performs this separation automatically and predicts three flux maps, in a standard format directly usable by mass-modelling pipelines. The network uses a hybrid architecture, pairing a vision transformer encoder reused from the lens detection network with a parallel convolutional branch. A custom loss function penalises both flux and structural error, while allowing independent weighting per component. From the network's predictions we also derive the modelling centre, fitting region, conjugate points, and binary maps, providing all the inputs that a modelling pipeline needs to run without manual setup. We evaluate the efficiency of the network on DR1 systems against existing fits and through expert validation. Across approximately 3000 inspected systems, taking 75% agreement between inspectors, just 39 lens models fail because of a segmentation error. Following this validation, the network provides the component separation and modelling setup for every system modelled in DR1. With automated segmentation, lens modelling is no longer limited by manual preprocessing and can now operate at the same scale as lens discovery.         |

|**Abstract**|            The European Space Agency's Euclid mission has issued its first major data release(Data Release 1, DR1), providing a transformative view of the extragalactic sky through deep photometry and near-infrared slitless spectroscopy. This paper presents the Euclid DR1 spectroscopic data set and details the evolution of the spectroscopic processing function (SPE PF) pipeline, which is responsible for the automated analysis of 1-dimensional spectra acquired by the Near-Infrared Spectro-Photometer (NISP). The SPE PF pipeline workflow comprises spectroscopic classification, redshift determination, and the systematic measurement of spectral features. Emission line fluxes are derived using two independent methodologies: direct integration (DI) and Gaussian fitting (GF). For sources exhibiting broad H$\alpha$ profiles (velocity dispersion larger than $1500$ km s$^{-1}$), a dedicated `QSO mode' is implemented to robustly deconvolve nuclear and host galaxy components. The data set was validated by cross-matching Euclid results with external spectroscopic catalogues, specifically Dark Energy Spectroscopic Instrument (DESI) DR1, and through an extensive visual inspection campaign of approximately 4000 sources within the Euclid Deep Field North (EDF-N). For high-quality samples selected in the EDF-N, we find a 98.5\% agreement between Euclid and DESI spectroscopic redshifts. This success rate increases to 98.9\% when incorporating pipeline probability and reliability estimators. (abridged)         |
