| [![arXiv](https://img.shields.io/badge/arXiv-2610.13758-b31b1b.svg)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.13758) | **A dust-reddened quasar in a [C II]-luminous host at z=6.55**  |
|| D. Yang, et al. -- incl., <mark>E. Bañados</mark> |
|*Appeared on*| *2026-10-12*|
|*Comments*| *11 figures, 2 tables, including appendices*|
|**Abstract**|            Most known quasars at $z>6$ were selected through their rest-frame UV emission. Only a small number of reddened quasars have been identified at these redshifts, leaving their nuclear and host-galaxy properties poorly constrained. We present JWST/NIRSpec spectroscopy and ALMA band 6 observations of the quasar J1440+0019 at $z=6.5488$, revealing its dust-reddened nature and jointly characterizing its nucleus and host galaxy. The continuum is best described by an SMC-like extinction law with $E(B-V)=0.20\pm0.03$. Three absorption systems detected in N V, Si IV, and C IV are blueshifted by approximately $900$--$3600$ km s$^{-1}$ and likely trace an ionized nuclear outflow. The broad Mg II line and extinction-corrected continuum yield a black-hole mass of $\log(M_{\rm BH}/M_\odot)=9.04\pm0.42$, a bolometric luminosity of $L_{\rm bol}\simeq1.1\times10^{47}$ erg s$^{-1}$, and an Eddington ratio of $\lambda_{\rm Edd}\simeq0.8$. ALMA observations further reveal its host galaxy to be among the most [C II]-luminous known at $z>6$, with $L_{\mathrm{[C II]}}=(7.21\pm0.25)\times10^9 L_\odot$. For a fiducial dust temperature of 47 K and emissivity index $\beta=1.6$, the dust continuum implies $L_{\rm TIR}=8.9\times10^{12} L_\odot$ and $M_{\rm dust}=4.0\times10^8 M_\odot$. The inferred dynamical mass places J1440+0019 in the region of the $M_{\rm BH}$--$M_{\rm dyn}$ plane occupied by high-redshift blue quasars. These observations reveal a moderately reddened, rapidly accreting quasar in a luminous, dust-rich host during reionization. Its measured nuclear and host-galaxy properties provide an observational reference for identifying and characterizing similar systems in future systematic searches with wide-area infrared surveys.         |

|| <mark>J. Li</mark>, J. P. U. Fynbo |
|*Appeared on*| *2026-10-12*|
|*Comments*| *Submitted to A&A. 12 pages, 31 figures*|
|**Abstract**|            We aim to provide a cookbook for purely astrometric selection of quasars with Gaia DR3 and to provide an example of its use for the identification of very luminous $z>2$ quasars. We analyse the Gaia DR3 astrometry of confirmed quasars and stationary sources. We show that the proper motion signal to noise $\mu/\sigma_{\mu}$ follows a Rayleigh-like distribution. We also show the importance of properly taking into account the correlation between proper motions in right ascension and declination, as well as the non-linear error propagation. We adopt the Mahalanobis distance to the null proper-motion hypothesis. A scaled $\chi^2$ distribution with $k=2$ and scale $s=1.1485$ is fitted to Gaia-CRF3; the corresponding $\pm2\sigma$ Gaussian-equivalent threshold is $D^2_{M(\mu)}<7.097$. The final criteria combine this with the parallax condition and $\mathrm{ruwe}<1.4$. Applying the new criteria to the SDSS DR16Q confirmed quasars, the recovery rate is $90.25\%$. The suggested purely astrometric selection significantly improves both completeness and purity compared to other strategies used in the literature, reaching a quasar purity of approximately $95\%$ ($\sim2\sigma$ Gaussian-equivalent). It can be used to improve the selection purity and completeness for future quasar surveys. As an example, we use the suggested strategi to locate 24 very bright $z>2$ quasars without redshifts in the literature. Our proposed astrometric selection strategy recovers 95\% of known quasars. Its potential use for new discoveries is demonstrated by the identification of 24 very luminous $z>2$ quasars. Ten of these are new discoveries and for an additional seven we correct the redshifts determined by other methods.}         |

|*Appeared on*| *2026-10-12*|
|*Comments*| *17 pages, 13 figures*|
|**Abstract**|            Primordial overdensities at small scales can be significantly larger than those observed at large scales. As a result, they can collapse much earlier, at redshifts $z \sim 100$. In these collapsed halos, stars and black holes can form, where they can then contribute to an early reionization of the universe. If this epoch is temporary, the free electrons will recombine yielding a low level of ionization leading into cosmic dawn. This contributes to the optical depth of the universe, and adds more weakly to low-$\ell$ polarization signals of ionization compared to later sources. We study the effects of a redshift-localized source of reionization $\Delta \tau_{burst}$ in the context of a broad suite of cosmological data to constrain this scenario, finding a preference of approximately $\sim 2-2.5\sigma$ for a new contribution. Regardless of Planck reprocessing used (PR3 - SRoll2+plik or PR4 - LoLLiPoP+CamSpec/HiLLiPoP), the results are consistent, favoring a new contribution $\Delta \tau_{burst} \sim 0.012$ ($0.001 < \Delta \tau_{burst} < 0.022$ at 95\%) and a similar total optical depth $\tau \sim 0.073$. The precise era of reionization is somewhat discrepant depending on the higher-$\ell$ reprocessing used, with HiLLiPoP favoring $z\sim 45$, CamSpec $z\sim 100$ and plik $z\sim 165$. If such a source is present at $40\lesssim z\lesssim150$, LiteBIRD should be able to detect it, while Simons Observatory should also constrain it.         |

|**Abstract**|            Emission-line galaxies provide key diagnostics of star formation, chemical enrichment, and AGN activity across cosmic time. We present a systematic census of emission-line sources using JWST/NIRCam wide-field slitless spectroscopy (WFSS) from the COSMOS-3D program, which covers a large contiguous area of 0.33 deg$^2$ and provides F444W grism spectroscopy over $3.9$--$5.1\ \mu$m. By combining slitless spectroscopy with deep multi-band imaging data, we construct a homogeneous spectroscopic sample of 12,275 objects at redshift 0--9. The typical $3\sigma$ line-flux limit reaches $3.2\times10^{-18}\ {\rm erg\ s^{-1}\ cm^{-2}}$. To address source confusion inherent to slitless spectroscopy, we develop a multi-step identification framework that combines information from spectra, images, spectral energy distributions, and spatial distributions. We introduce convolutional neural network and large language model to emulate the human identification process and improve efficiency. The resulting catalog provides spectroscopic redshifts, emission-line measurements, and derived physical properties for all sources. The F444W WFSS enables the detection of multiple emission lines, including primarily [O III], H$\alpha$, He I $\lambda10830$, [S III] $\lambda\lambda9531,9069$, Pa$\beta$, Pa$\alpha$, Br$\beta$, PAH, Br$\alpha$, allowing a continuous census of galaxies across cosmic time. We construct composite spectra in several redshift bins and detect a variety of faint spectral features that are difficult to identify in individual spectra, including higher-order hydrogen recombination lines, CO absorption bands, and [Fe II] lines. This catalog provides a key reference dataset for studies of galaxy evolution and AGN activity, and demonstrates the power of WFSS for constructing large, unbiased spectroscopic samples over wide areas. The full catalog will be publicly available online upon publication.         |
