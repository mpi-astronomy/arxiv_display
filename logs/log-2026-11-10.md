|**Abstract**|            The deuterium-to-hydrogen ratio (D/H) has provided key insights into the origin and evolution of the Solar System. |**Abstract**|            Classical Cepheids (CCs) are key distance indicators and tracers of young stellar populations in the Milky Way. Their period-luminosity-metallicity (PLZ) relation also makes them calibrators of the Hubble constant $H_0$. Despite their importance, chemical composition, especially non-LTE (NLTE) effects, remains a significant source of systematics in PLZ applications. In preparation for the large CCs sample expected from the 4MIDABLE-HR (4MOST Consortium Survey 4: Milky Way Disc and Bulge High-Resolution) survey, we present a method for deriving Cepheid atmospheric parameters and chemical abundances in NLTE. Using the Payne artificial neural network (ANN) trained on NLTE spectra, we derive atmospheric parameters and abundances for 17 elements: O, Na, Mg, Al, Si, Ca, Ti, Mn, Fe, Co, Ni, Sr, Y, Ba, Eu in NLTE and C and Cr in LTE. The resulting abundance distributions and gradients align well with Galactic disk expectations from chemical evolution models. We show that our NLTE astrophysical analysis yields robust solutions for atmospheric parameters and chemical abundances across spectra at the resolving power of 4MOST-HR. |**Abstract**|            GQ Lup B is a massive (10-40 M$_{\text{Jup}}$) companion orbiting the young T-Tauri star GQ Lup A at a projected distance of $\sim$110 au (0.7''). It is outside the circumprimary disk in the submm continuum, but its ${\sim}\,63^\circ$ inclined orbit intersects the primary's gas disk twice per period. We report a 5.8$\sigma$ detection of continuum emission at the location of GQ Lup B, obtained with the Atacama Large Millimeter/submillimeter Array (ALMA) at 0.89 mm (band 7). Assuming a gas to dust ratio of 100, our results indicate a very low disk mass to accretion rate ratio, of the order of $\sim10^4$ years, analogously to the highest accretors in the stellar mass regime. | [![arXiv](https://img.shields.io/badge/arXiv-2611.12581-b31b1b.svg)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.12581) | **The compositional link between rocky exoplanets and their host stars is consistent with Earth-like devolatilization**  |
|| <mark>Y. Reinarz</mark>, L. Acuña-Aguirre, H. S. Wang |
|*Appeared on*| *2026-11-10*|
|*Comments*| *15 pages, 9 figures, 7 tables. Accepted for publication in Astronomy & Astrophysics*|
|**Abstract**|            Previous work found a higher bulk iron-to-silicon (Fe/Si) ratio in rocky exoplanets relative to their host star, traditionally explained by iron enrichment. An alternative explanation is devolatilization of the planetary abundance ratios with respect to the host star, where volatile elements such as O and S are progressively depleted compared to refractory elements such as Fe and Mg, as in Earth. We explore whether these elemental molar ratios are consistent with a devolatilization trend. We revisit the core mass fraction of 14 rocky exoplanets with masses between 0.5 and 10 Earth masses whose host stars have spectroscopic abundances of Fe, Si, Mg, O, and S in the literature. We use interior structure retrievals with two independent forward models, the Marseille Super-Earth Interior model and pyExoInt, to estimate compositional parameters, including elemental molar ratios, from mass and radius measurements. We find that 8 out of 14 exoplanets in our sample present an Earth-like devolatilization trend, while only one planet shows a 1:1 relationship between planetary and stellar abundances. This suggests that devolatilization offers a plausible explanation for the observed non-1:1 relation. The two interior models agree on the devolatilization trend, demonstrating that our derived trends are robust. The main limiting factor in constraining refractory compositions of rocky planets relative to their host star is the small sample with constrained stellar abundances and the lack of variety in orbital distances. The PLATO mission will help by extending the sample of characterized rocky planets to orbital periods of up to one year. The variety in refractory elemental ratio trends may indicate a wide diversity in bulk composition, outgassing and other mantle properties relevant for habitability.         |
