|**Abstract**|            We present an updated analysis of the JWST coronagraphic imaging data of the HIP 65426 system. We aim to search for and characterize potential planetary companions as well as provide updated astrometry and photometry for the known exoplanet HIP 65426 b following updates to the JWST and spaceKLIP pipelines. Our measured photometry and astrometry show significant differences to those in Carter et al. One galaxy in particular appears as a point source in MIRI but is highly extended in NIRCam and exposes a potential drawback to single-epoch MIRI imaging in a blind search for companions.         |

|| J. Hyden, et al. -- incl., <mark>M. Benisty</mark> |
|*Appeared on*| *2026-11-06*|
|*Comments*| *Accepted for publication on ApJ*|
|**Abstract**|            DQ Tau is a TTauri eccentric binary with pulsed accretion bursts regulated by its $\sim 16$-day orbital period. We have monitored this system with seven instruments from ground and space over 40 days. We present results on molecular gas variability from nine JWST-MIRI and nine IRTF-iSHELL epochs over three sequential accretion bursts with accretion luminosity pulses increasing by factors of $\sim 9$-80. A fundamental new measurement from this analysis is the line broadening of CO (from iSHELL) and H$_2$O (from MIRI) lines, which we use to estimate Keplerian radii and their variability in parallel to the line luminosity. This unprecedented dataset clearly shows that the molecular emission does not react like HI and the 5-$\mu$m continuum with each accretion burst: the line luminosity and width of all hot molecular gas tracers decrease over $\sim30$-35 days, consistent with an emitting region decreasing in temperature and mass as it recedes to larger radii within the $\sim 0.2$-0.4 au inner cavity perturbed by the binary. The infrared molecular spectrum of DQ Tau is affected more by disk dynamics then by chemical changes triggered by variable accretion, supporting model expectations for chemical relaxation timescales that are much longer than the duration of these accretion bursts ($\gg 10$ days). The variability observed in all tracers in this study is consistent with a scenario where gas mass builds up near the disk edge to modulate the burst peak intensity and gas density in the inner cavity every $\sim4$-5 orbital periods. The origin of this modulation is still unclear.         |

|*Appeared on*| *2026-11-06*|
|*Comments*| *6 pages, 5 figures. Accepted for publication in Astronomy & Astrophysics*|
|**Abstract**|            Space-based astrometric missions such as Hipparcos and Gaia rely on one-dimensional scanning strategies that couple the measured signals with both observation times and scanning geometry. This coupling can introduce spurious periodicities and complicate their interpretation. We present a non-parametric extension of the phase-distance correlation (PDC) periodogram that quantifies and reduces dependence on scan geometry at the period-search stage. Using Hipparcos data, we identified recurrent periodicities associated with characteristic scanning-law timescales and showed that they are strongly suppressed by the scan-angle-controlled statistic. We then applied the method to Gaia Data Release 4 (DR4) prerelease epoch astrometry, where a recurrent ~54-day feature is suppressed across multiple sources while independently known orbital signals remain detectable. The method provides a diagnostic of scan-angle dependence and a way to mitigate its impact on period searches. These results are particularly relevant for Gaia DR4, where public epoch astrometry will enable large-scale searches for companions ranging from exoplanets to dormant black holes.         |

|**Abstract**|            The interstellar medium of massive quiescent galaxies holds key clues to how they quench, yet remain poorly understood. Although dust is expected to be depleted rapidly ($\lesssim$100 Myr) after quenching, some quiescent galaxies host substantial dust reservoirs, raising the question of whether dust survives quenching or is replenished afterward. We present ALMA Band 6 continuum observations ($\lambda_{\mathrm{rest}}$$>$250$\mu$m) of eight strongly-lensed, spectroscopically-selected quiescent galaxies at z$\sim$1-2.5 from the COOL-LAMPS and REQUIEM surveys, plus five archival REQUIEM systems.
