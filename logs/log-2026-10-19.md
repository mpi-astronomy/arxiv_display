| [![arXiv](https://img.shields.io/badge/arXiv-2610.22503-b31b1b.svg)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.22503) | **An ALMA view on molecular complexity: water, methanol, and methyl formate in the HD 100546 and IRS 48 disks**  |
|| L. Rampinelli, et al. -- incl., <mark>M. Benisty</mark> |
|*Appeared on*| *2026-10-19*|
|*Comments*| *Accepted for publication in the Astrophysical Journal. 21 pages, 9 figures, 3 tables*|
|**Abstract**|            Water and methanol are the fundamental constituents of interstellar and cometary ices: understanding their abundances in disks is therefore of high importance to reveal how this ice reservoir evolves across the timeline of star and planet formation. We present ALMA band 5 line emission observations of water and methanol in the warm Herbig disks of HD 100546 and IRS 48. While the IRS 48 disk is methanol-rich, water vapor is not detected, implying a high methanol-to-water column density ratio >0.2, possibly reflecting a distinct ice composition. Methanol and water are both detected in the HD 100546 disk, leading to a CH3OH/H2O ratio of ~0.02, consistent with the median of the cometary distribution and with the lower edge of the distribution of Young Stellar Objects. We suggest that the gas-phase methanol-to-water ratio in the HD 100546 disk directly reveals the ice reservoir subsequently sublimated in the warm disk stage. Moreover, we report a methyl formate-to-methanol ratio in the IRS 48 disk of ~0.2, consistent with the upper edge of the distribution of Young Stellar Objects. The methyl formate-to-methanol ratio in the HD 100546 disk is ~2, much higher than in younger and older sources, possibly reflecting a chemical enhancement of gas-phase methyl formate. We additionally constrain non-LTE conditions, optical depths, and emitting regions of the methanol lines in both disks. These results offer a new opportunity to probe the chemical trail of molecular complexity in the intermediate disk stage, bridging early and late phases of the star and planet formation history.         |

|**Abstract**|            How does a star's birth environment set the mass, angular momentum and chemical complexity delivered to its planet-forming disk, and thereby whether it builds planets, brown dwarfs or stellar companions? This requires following material from the cloud to the sub-au to few-au scales where infalling gas, magnetized winds and the disk interact, across all star-forming environments. At frequencies where young disks are optically thin, current facilities, even ALMA after its Wideband Sensitivity Upgrade, fall short by a factor of a few in angular resolution and an order of magnitude in sensitivity. With three times finer angular resolution and ten times the sensitivity, ALMA2040 will follow the path of material from cloud to disk across the full stellar-mass spectrum and, for the first time, measure how this path changes with environment: resolving the sub-au infall-impact and wind-launching zones nearby, disk fragmentation and the onset of multiplicity in distant high-mass regions, and disk formation at a few tenths of solar metallicity.
