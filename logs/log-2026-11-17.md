|*Appeared on*| *2026-11-17*|
|*Comments*| **|
|**Abstract**|            Deriving stellar astrophysical parameters from large-scale spectroscopic surveys requires accurate modeling of spectra across a wide wavelength coverage and a broad range of stellar parameter space, which remains a great challenge. In this work, we present \texttt{TDSM}, a physically motivated neural network architecture that self-consistently emulates both the flux and its first-order derivative of the ab initio stellar spectra. The method enables accurate and precise emulation of Kurucz synthetic spectra with severely blended features at $R \simeq 2000$ in a high-dimensional parameter space, achieving a typical pixel-wise flux precision of 0.05\% in the wavelength range $3700<\lambda<9000$Å over the parameter range of $3000 < T_{\rm eff} < 45,000$~K, $-1 < \log g < 5$, and $-4.0 < \mathrm{[Fe/H]} < 0.5$. We conduct detailed refinement and calibration efforts to mitigate systematic errors, which are revealed to be significant and complex due to imperfections in the ab initio model atmosphere. This adds a valuable dataset for studies of Galactic structure and archaeology, stellar populations, and related areas of astrophysics.         |

|| P. Kumar, et al. -- incl., <mark>A. Pillepich</mark> |
|*Appeared on*| *2026-11-17*|
|*Comments*| *Accepted for publication in A&A on 27/10/2026*|
|**Abstract**|            Stellar orbits encode the dynamical history of galaxies and provide a physically motivated link between galaxy structures and assembly processes. Although orbit-based decompositions are well established in the Local Universe, their evolution across cosmic time, especially at higher redshifts, remains poorly explored. We investigate the orbital structure of \texttt{LEGA-C-like} galaxies in the \texttt{TNG100} simulation from $z=1$ to $z=0$ to identify how stellar mass, star formation activity, environment, merger history, gas content, and ex situ stellar mass growth shape their orbital transformation. We decomposed the stellar orbital structure of simulated galaxies into cold, warm, hot, and counter-rotating (CR) components using time-averaged orbital circularities. We find a strong mass dependence of orbital structure at $z=0$ as well as at $z=1$. Galaxies below $\log(M_\star/M_\odot)\sim11.2$ have a combined warm and cold orbital fraction of $\sim70\%$, whereas more massive systems have a hot-orbit fraction of $\sim60\%$. These trends are consistent with assembly pathways driven predominantly by wet and dry major mergers, respectively. Tracking the same galaxies to $z=0$ reveals a systematic, but mass-dependent, evolution toward lower rotational support: At both low and high stellar masses, the hot-orbit fraction increases, whereas the relative change is greater in low-mass galaxies. The survival of cold structure depends strongly on the star formation state at $z=1$; star-forming progenitors retain substantially larger cold fractions than the quiescent progenitors. At both redshifts, the orbital fractions show a rise (decline) in hot and CR (cold and warm) orbits with the ex situ stellar mass fraction at a fixed stellar mass. These results provide the first evolutionary framework for interpreting orbit-based decompositions of galaxies at the \texttt{LEGA-C} epoch.         |

|| <mark>S. Shahaf</mark>, et al. -- incl., <mark>K. El-Badry</mark>, <mark>H.-W. Rix</mark>, <mark>R. Andrae</mark> |
|*Appeared on*| *2026-11-17*|
|*Comments*| *Submitted to A&A. 17 pages, 8 figures. Comments are welcome. Code available at this https URL*|
|**Abstract**|            Gaia has observed every detected source on the sky 50 to 100 times, each time through a window of about 1 by 2 arcseconds at a different scan angle. The resulting one-dimensional flux profiles to be released in Gaia DR4 encode the two-dimensional arrangement of nearby point sources at ~0.1 arcsecond resolution. Repeated observations also constrain their relative motion. We present a method to model the scene with a mixture of moving point sources. Complementing Gaia DPAC's source-environment reconstruction, our approach focuses on close, blended companions and models their relative motion. We first fit each window with a few point sources and obtain their along-scan positions. We then triangulate these positions from different scan angles to identify candidate sources on the sky. A joint forward model of all windows measures positions, fluxes, relative proper motions, and parallax differences, even for blended components. In simulations, we recover companions at separations smaller than the instrumental profile width. For a moving companion at ~75 mas separation with a flux ratio of ~0.2, we obtain a separation precision of ~0.1 mas and a relative proper-motion precision of ~0.1 mas/yr per component, even in a scene contaminated by a third source. These precisions assume a known instrumental profile and independent Gaussian noise. We also recover differential parallax for a chance-aligned source. Modeling nearby contaminants prevents significant biases. With a Gaia-like instrumental profile, sampling and scan law, we show a sensitivity of ~5 mag in contrast at separations of 50-150 mas, and we discuss its dependence on the color term of Gaia's line-spread function. The method could extend the census of close companions and provide relative astrometry, and so dynamical information, along short orbital arcs. External measurements could also guide retrospective searches in Gaia's windows.         |

|**Abstract**|            The nature of progenitor systems of Type Ia supernovae (SNe Ia) and their explosion mechanism remain controversial. Radio observations provide a powerful probe of the interaction between SN ejecta and circumstellar material (CSM), offering important constraints on the mass-loss histories of their progenitor systems. We present extensive Jansky Very Large Array (JVLA) observations of SN 2020eyj at 1.5-10 GHz, tracing its radio emission to approximately 1300 days after discovery and revealing, for the first time, the long-term radio evolution of an SN Ia. The radio light curves of SN 2020eyj exhibit a relatively flat evolution at early epochs, followed by a gradual long-term decline, while its radio spectra show a power-law behavior with a low-frequency turnover. Combined with the optical spectral signatures of SN 2020eyj, our radio observations provide strong evidence for substantial helium-rich mass loss from the progenitor system prior to explosion. The inferred CSM properties pose significant challenges to existing SN Ia progenitor models, although the ejection of a common envelope shortly before explosion may offer a plausible explanation.         |

|**Abstract**|            Strong gravitational-lensing systems with multiple source planes are powerful tools for probing the stellar and dark matter content of galaxies. Lens modelling can measure the relative Einstein radii of the two source planes, which encode a ratio of angular-diameter distances determined by the lens and source redshifts and the dark energy equation of state, independently of the Hubble constant. Galaxy-scale double-source-plane lenses (DSPLs) are extremely rare. We present a sample of 84 galaxy-scale DSPL candidates in Euclid Data Release 1 (DR1), of which 80 are newly identified. Among our lens candidates, 11 form the 'Gold' sample: each shows two sets of multiple images at different radii, and the observed configuration is reproduced by a DSPL lens model.
