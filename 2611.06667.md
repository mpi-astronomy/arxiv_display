<div id="abstract">

**Abstract:** We demonstrate the efficacy of crowd-sourced refinement of strong gravitational lens candidate samples by citizen scientists. Binary (lens/non-lens) classification by citizen scientists has been conducted previously, and here we extend such categorisation into `definite', `probable', `possible', and `non-lenses'. This grading is a required stage ahead of any strong lens sample publication and is usually carried out by (small) teams of researchers.We compare the responses of the volunteers to those of strong lens researchers and discuss the optimal strategy for lens classification and grading in future strong lens searches. Volunteers are found to be able to correctly identify systems of each grade, and their responses can be calibrated to be comparable to those of researchers.Grade posteriors, defined as the probability that a strong lens system classified by the volunteers is a certain grade, converge most rapidly for non-lens systems. We find that one researcher classification is equivalent to only $\rt{1.9}$ volunteer classifications when grading such systems.There is significant disagreement in the grading of intermediate-grade lens candidates amongst both volunteers and researchers. Moving towards calibrated lens probabilities for future lens searches would help to account for such subjectivity. If the Q1 lens search was rerun with volunteers performing lens grading (and corresponding calibration), a completeness of $79\%$ for Grades A+B (corresponding to definite + probable lenses respectively) could be achieved with $41\%$ purity prior to researcher inspection -- this would correspond to an expected $12 500$ true lenses (i.e. excluding contaminants) in Euclid DR1.
